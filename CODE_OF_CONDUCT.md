# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying, argue about the code rather than the person, and
accept that a maintainer may say no to good work for reasons of scope.

## Scope

This applies to every project space: issues, pull requests, commit messages, reviews, discussion
threads, and anywhere someone is speaking as a representative of Apollo Studios.

## Expected

- Technical criticism with a reason attached. "This allocates per sample, which is why the render
  halves" beats "this is bad".
- Patience with people who do not know the codebase yet. Every regular here was new once.
- Tolerance for being wrong, including when it is you.
- Credit where it is due, in public, in plain words.
- Keeping a review to the patch. A formatting opinion is not a referendum on someone's skill.

## Not expected

- Personal attacks, or reviewing the author instead of the diff.
- Condescension: "obviously", "just", "as anyone knows".
- Re-litigating a settled decision without new information.
- Long unrequested rewrites of someone's pull request.
- Hostile bumping. A polite ping after a week is fine; three in a day is not.

## Harassment

Beyond ordinary rudeness, these count as harassment: sustained off-topic criticism after being
asked to stop, repeated contact in private channels, deliberate misgendering, and dismissal of a
contribution on grounds unrelated to the work.

## Standards at a glance

| Situation | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Someone's patch has a real flaw | Point at the line, name the consequence, suggest the alternative |
| Someone's patch breaks a rule in CONTRIBUTING | Link the rule, say which one |
| You already decided to reject it | Say so early, with the reason, so nobody polishes a dead patch |
| You made a mistake | Say it, fix it, move on. No essay of apology |
| Disagreement stalls | Ask what evidence would change the other's mind; if there is none, take it to an issue |

## Enforcement

The maintainer, Jackson Davis, is the arbiter. Reports go to the issue tracker or directly to the
maintainer, whichever the reporter prefers; a private report is treated as private until the
reporter says otherwise.

Responses scale with the problem and are applied in this order where the behaviour repeats:

1. **Note** — a comment in the thread, no other change.
2. **Warning** — a named summary of what needs to change, restated in the thread.
3. **Cooldown** — a short period where the person may read and react but not open new work.
4. **Removal** — commit access or triage rights withdrawn.

Serious one-off cases can jump straight to step 3 or 4. Everything starts from a clean slate each
time unless the same behaviour recurs.

## Appeals

Say so once, in the same thread, with a reason. The maintainer either reverses or explains. There
is no committee and no second round.

## Attribution

scope, expected, not expected, enforcement ladder — is deliberately conventional; the wording is
